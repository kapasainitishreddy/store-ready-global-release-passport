# Prior IBM Bob Hackathon research

Research checked September 25, 2026. The prior May 2026 IBM Bob Hackathon had 503 submissions, 5,628 participants, and a listed $10,000 total prize pool. Lablab's final dashboard names Pedigree (CtrlCats) as champion, Atlas as runner-up, and Sandbox — Castles Crumble. Fix Them First. (404) in third place. The dashboard does not publish a prize split. Winner/participant posts report $5,000 for Pedigree and $3,000 for Atlas; no primary source found in this research confirms the third-place award amount, so this package does not infer it.

## What stood out

- **Pedigree, champion:** a verifiable provenance passport for AI-assisted commits, with signed attestations, a CI gate, a custom IBM Bob mode, and MCP tools. Its core workflow made IBM Bob part of the product's engineering loop instead of mentioning it only in the presentation.
- **Atlas, runner-up:** a deterministic interactive city map for repository architecture and hotspots, which turned codebase understanding into something visible and quickly demoable.
- **Sandbox, third:** a no-key demo mode with a visual failure replay that made an abstract risk analysis easy to experience. The submission claimed deep Bob-assisted generation; in our own entry, we must prove our actual sessions with screenshots rather than echoing unverified claims.
- **Other top-voted projects:** LegacyLink AI generated a full modernization scaffold with tests/docs; RepoMind focused narrowly on repository onboarding; CodeAtlas surfaced dependency confidence and analysis limitations. Those are strong signals for a concrete vertical workflow, exportable artifacts, and visible uncertainty.

## What we should do

1. Keep the product promise narrow enough to prove: per-app, per-market launch readiness, with localized listing work and security/compliance evidence linked to that app.
2. Show one working vertical slice: choose app → choose country/locale → prepare a store/SEO plan → inspect a source-backed security finding → use Bob to make a real reviewed code change → rerun a reproducible check.
3. Make Bob part of real development work. Use it on a focused issue, capture the true diff and test output, and retain each member's authentic Bob session-summary screenshot.
4. Make one visual moment memorable: compare the same product across country/store plans and show exactly which locale copy/assets/evidence is incomplete.
5. Prioritize reproducibility and proof over claims of global completeness. The current prototype offers a searchable CLDR catalogue of 257 countries and territories with planning templates and 585 locale suggestions; it does not verify worldwide law or store coverage, live keyword data, translation, or store publishing.
